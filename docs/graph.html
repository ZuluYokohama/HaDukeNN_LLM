G1c pass is compute-matched, not a per-byte modeling win. g1d beats hnt at equal data. g1g is pending.

Primary comparisons

  • hnt vs equal-size control. G1: hnt64k against sw64k, the equal-size superword-BPE control, at one vocabulary size.
  • best arm vs struct32k / GPT-2. G1 holds hnt64k to struct32k on test BPB and train FLOPs per byte. H1 keeps GPT-2 BPE on the same train bytes. G4 holds the full arm to the tuned baseline at matched all-in FLOPs.
  • pe_heat vs baseline. P2 holds the heat and heat+RoPE positional encodings to the tuned modern baseline.

Highlighting a gate does not mean the campaign passed it. G0 is partial because V=65,536 is over 64,000. G1 has failed. G1b, the structured hnt40k follow-up, has also failed on BPB; it is not a separate highlighted node. G1c passes only as a compute-matched comparison on WikiText-103, n=3: hnt40k-struct-v2 test bits per byte 1.269376 ± 0.004417 at 1.2061e7 train FLOPs per byte and 4.2006e15 train FLOPs, sw40k-struct-v2 control 1.322698 ± 0.006710, struct32k 1.322354, GPT-2 tokenizer arm 1.328219. hnt versus that control is -0.0533 (p 7.1e-4). g1d's data-matched struct32k, 348.3M bytes, is 1.194155 ± 0.000824 at 1.0756e16 train FLOPs and beats hnt by 0.075 at equal data, so the G1c pass is a compute-efficiency win, not a per-byte modeling win. g1g, the FLOPs-matched hnt run at 1.0756e16, is pending and has no result. Caveats: n=3, one model size, WikiText-103 only, 39.9M versus 23.2M total parameters, same 10.62M non-embedding. Later gates are not run.
